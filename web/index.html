Rank candidate genes from a patient’s phenotypes, and see how sure the ranking is.

Genoarch compares Human Phenotype Ontology terms with every disease profile in its index of HPO annotations, turns the match into a calibrated probability over diseases and genes, and tells you when no known disease fits better than chance.

                About Genoarch

                Genoarch is a research tool for undiagnosed disease work. It turns a patient’s phenotype description, written in Human Phenotype Ontology terms, into ranked candidate diseases and genes, states how confident that ranking is, and suggests what to examine next.

                The analysis engine is open source under the MIT license. The same engine runs in Python behind the API and in JavaScript in this page, and both give the same ranking for the same input.

                Legal

                Research use only

                Genoarch is not a medical device. It is not intended for diagnosis, treatment, screening or any clinical decision, and its output must not be used to make or confirm a diagnosis.

                Rankings reflect similarity between phenotype terms and published annotations. They can be wrong, incomplete or biased by gaps in the annotation data. Gene hypotheses are leads for research, not evidence of causality. The reported accuracy and calibration come from solved phenopacket cases (Phenopacket Store 0.1.27) used as a retrospective proxy for undiagnosed cases; no open cohort of truly undiagnosed patients is available to us, so these figures describe solved, published cases.

                Privacy

                In this page the analysis runs entirely in your browser: the phenotype terms you enter are not sent anywhere. The page loads its typefaces from Google Fonts. When the optional server mode is used, terms are sent to the Genoarch API for the request only and are not stored by the application.

                Data sources

                This product uses the Human Phenotype Ontology (hpo.jax.org). The release versions in use are shown in the page footer and on the Method page. Term labels, synonyms and relationships are used without alteration, as the HPO license terms require.

                • Human Phenotype Ontology and HPO annotations, used unchanged.
                • Disease identifiers and names from OMIM, Orphanet and DECIPHER as distributed in the HPO annotation file.
                • GA4GH Phenopacket Store, used only to fit and evaluate calibration; no case data is included in this site.
